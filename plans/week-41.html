Why run-first is the right call now

Running economy and durability — the stuff that gets you through 26.2 miles — come from running often and easy, not from a second hard ride. Three easy runs a week beats two for your marathon, and the one Tuesday ride keeps your aerobic cross-training and the fun without the recovery cost of two Zone 4 group rides.

🏃 3 runs — all Zone 2
Two easy mid-week runs + the long run. Every one ≤136 bpm. This is where the marathon engine is built. Hold the cadence (155+ when running).
🚴 1 ride — controlled
Tuesday 6 AM. Enjoy it, spin 80+, but don't chase every surge into Zone 4. Sit in, ride nearer the front to cut the accordion effect. Aerobic, not a race.
📊

Your one metric this block: pace at HR 130

This is how you'll see your stamina improve. Right now you hold ~13:00/mi early then slow to a walk at HR 130. Over the next 6–8 weeks of consistent easy running, that same 130 bpm will hold a running pace deeper into the run — that's your aerobic base growing, and it's the whole game. Don't chase it by running harder now; let it come from easy volume. Check it on the Monday run each week.

🦵

Protect the knee as running volume steps up

Going from 2 to 3 runs is the right progression, but it's also where the old injury pattern lurks — so the guardrails stay firm: no two run days back-to-back (built into the schedule), ice the right knee 15 min after every run, glute primer before each, cadence 155+. Once you bank 4 pain-free weeks at 3 runs, we add a 4th easy run. Any knee pain ≥3/10 → stop and tell me.

🥗

Fuel the long run — it softens the fade

Part of your late-run fade is fuel, not just fitness. Saturday: eat before (oats + banana + honey), and take carbs during from minute 20 — a few dates or half a banana every ~20 min, plus electrolytes. Then a proper carb+protein meal within 45 min after (eggs + rice + curd, or chicken + rice). Full fueling guide coming as a reference page if you want it.
