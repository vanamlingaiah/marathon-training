🚴 X Lab RS5 — First-Week Setup Guide

Three things to get right before and during the first rides. The clipless drill is non-negotiable; the fit check protects your knee.

1 · Clipless drill (Monday evening)
Lean against a wall or doorway (or put the bike on the trainer). Clip in and out 20 times each foot — twist heel OUT to release. Then a 10-min empty parking lot session: ride, stop, unclip, repeat. Unclip your lead foot 50m BEFORE every stop. Nearly every new clipless rider tips over once at a stop — the drill is how you skip that.
2 · Saddle height (knee protection)
Heel-on-pedal method: sit on the saddle, heel on pedal at the very bottom of the stroke — leg should be dead straight. Then with the ball of your foot on the pedal you get the correct slight bend. Front-of-knee ache = saddle too low (raise 3–5mm). Behind-knee = too high. Given the PFPS, take this seriously — a bike-shop fit is worth it if any discomfort persists.
3 · Cleat position
Ball of foot over the pedal spindle; cleats angled so your feet sit naturally (most people's heels sit slightly in). If your knee tracks oddly or aches, cleat angle is the first suspect. RC3 soles are stiff — expect the first rides to feel different from sneakers on the IC4.
⛔ Road rules that keep you safe
Helmet every ride, no exceptions · ride WITH traffic, predictably, signal turns · assume every car hasn't seen you · 6:15 AM = cool AND quiet roads (double win) · carry phone + ID + water · lights/bright colors help even in daylight · skip headphones outdoors for now — you need ears on traffic (save the Oakleys' audio for later, familiar routes).

⛽ Fuel Control & Heart-Rate Maintenance — the playbook

Everything that controls your fuel and your HR, in one place. Most of this you're already doing — this consolidates it into rules you can run on autopilot.

⛽ Fuel control
  • Wake: 16 oz water immediately, every day — before coffee, before food.
  • Pre-session (all): light carbs 20–45 min out — banana or ½ oats. Under 60-min sessions need nothing more.
  • During 60+ min sessions: 4–6 oz water + electrolyte every 15–20 min; optional gel at 45 min on the long run.
  • Texas heat sodium: 500–700mg sodium per hour of sweating — electrolyte mix, not plain water, on every outdoor session.
  • Post (30-min window): 20–30g protein + 40–60g carbs. The shake or oats+whey. Non-negotiable after long run and swim.
  • Calorie cycling continues: 🔴 full fuel Wed/Fri/Sat · 🔵 mild deficit Mon/Tue/Thu/Sun · protein 120–155g every day.
  • Never deficit around the long run — Friday and Saturday are always full-fuel days.
❤️ Heart-rate maintenance
  • Run the run-intervals GENTLY. The 128→140 climb should take 2–3+ minutes. If it takes 30 seconds, slow the interval down — that's the #1 fix.
  • Hydration is the cheapest HR reducer: mild dehydration adds 5–10 bpm at the same effort. The 16 oz on waking matters.
  • Cool = lower HR: every degree matters. The 6:15 AM window is worth 6–8 bpm vs 7:30.
  • Accept late-run drift: +3–5 bpm in the final third of a long run in July is physiology, not failure. The walk breaks manage it.
  • Outdoor bike HR will be spikier than Zwift — wind, rises, stops. Use 5-second-average HR on the watch, shift to an easier gear EARLY on any rise, and judge the ride by average HR, not the spikes.
  • Morning check: if resting HR is 7+ bpm above your normal, make it an easy day — that's fatigue or oncoming stress talking.
  • Sleep 8 hr: short sleep reliably raises next-day HR. It's a training metric, not a luxury.
