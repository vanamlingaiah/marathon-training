How the 6-pack actually happens

Everyone already has abs — whether they show depends almost entirely on the fat layer over them, and you can't spot-reduce. The lever is overall body fat, driven ~80% by nutrition. The great news: your marathon training is already an elite fat-burning engine. So we don't bolt on a separate fat-loss program — we let the running and biking do their work, tighten nutrition slightly, and add core work that builds the deep muscle so it's defined once the fat drops. Marathon-first means the abs arrive as a byproduct, not at the cost of your training.

🎯

On the lower-belly pooch specifically

It's the most stubborn storage area and the last to go — largely genetic, no exercise targets it directly. But two things genuinely improve its appearance: building the transverse abdominis (the deep corset muscle that flattens the lower abdomen) and fixing anterior pelvic tilt, which your 8-hour standing desk worsens and which makes the lower belly protrude. Both are built into this week's core protocol — dead bugs, hollow holds, and posterior pelvic tilt work, not endless crunches.

🍽️ Calorie Cycling — lean out without wrecking training

Marathon-first means we protect recovery. So we eat at maintenance on hard days and run a mild deficit on easy days. This nets a weekly deficit that reveals the lower belly over weeks, while never under-fueling the work that matters.

🔴 Hard Days — eat at maintenance
~2,600–2,900 kcal
Wed (cadence run), Sat (long run), + one bike day. Full carbs, full protein. Fuel the work, protect the knee, recover properly. Never deficit on these.
🔵 Easy Days — mild deficit
~2,300–2,500 kcal
Mon, Tue, Thu, Sun. ~250–300 below maintenance. High protein stays, trim refined carbs + added fats. Body taps fat stores when training demand is lower.

The two rules that matter most: (1) Keep protein high every day — 0.8–1g per lb bodyweight (~120–155g) preserves muscle in a deficit. (2) Never cut fuel around the long run. As race training intensifies later, we drop the deficit entirely.

The Core Protocol — built for the lower belly

Three sessions a week, woven into existing days (no extra training time). These build the deep stabilizers that flatten the midsection — not surface crunches that do little. Master the deep work first; visible definition follows once body fat drops via the nutrition side.

Dead-Bug
3 × 12/side · slow
The #1 transverse abdominis exercise. Keep lower back pressed flat to the floor — that's the deep muscle engaging. Directly targets lower-belly flattening.
Deep core · TVA
Hollow Hold
3 × 20–25 sec
Gymnast's core builder. Full anterior chain under tension. Builds the "compression" strength that pulls the abdominal wall in.
Deep core · compression
Pallof Press
3 × 10/side · band
Anti-rotation — resists the band pulling you sideways. Builds 360° core stability and protects your back from desk-sitting.
Anti-rotation
Plank + Side Plank
3 × 45 sec / 2 × 30 sec
Isometric foundation. Side plank specifically targets the obliques (the "love handle" line) and deep lateral stabilizers.
Isometric · obliques
Leg Raises
2 × 12 · controlled
The lower abs' main mover. Lower slowly, don't let the back arch. Hits exactly the area below the navel where the pooch sits.
Lower abs
Posterior Pelvic Tilt
2 × 15 · hold 3 sec
Counters the anterior tilt your standing desk causes. Reduces the lower-belly "protrusion" appearance and is the missing piece most people skip.
