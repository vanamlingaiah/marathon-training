🌊 Your run-walk method — validated by the data

I analyzed Wednesday's run minute-by-minute. Your pattern of running to 140 then walking to 128 is working exactly as intended — and it's a legitimate, coach-endorsed method for heat training. Here's the cycle, formalized:

🏃
RUN at 150 cadence
Hold your turnover. Let HR climb naturally as you run.
→
⬆️
HR hits 140
The trigger. Don't push past it — transition to walk.

🚶

Keep moving. HR drifts down as you recover.
→
⬇️
HR drops to 128
Recovery complete. Resume running. Repeat the cycle.

Why the run-walk method is the right call (not a compromise)

This is the Jeff Galloway method — used by hundreds of thousands of marathoners, including for full race distances. In Texas summer it's not just acceptable, it's optimal: it keeps you in true Zone 2, protects your PFPS knee from continuous loading, and lets you accumulate more total running volume than grinding out a slow continuous jog. As temps drop in fall, the walk breaks naturally shorten. You're not "failing to run continuously" — you're training smart.

🌅

One thing to protect: the morning runs (bikes can stay flexible)

Your runs moved earlier (Sat 6:40 AM — great), but Monday is still drifting to evening (6:23 PM at 85°F+). The bikes drifting to 8:30 AM is totally fine since they're indoor and climate-controlled. The priority: get all three runs into the 6:00–6:45 AM window. Monday especially — an evening run in the heat is why its pace (14:42) lagged the morning runs (13:18–13:30).

📊

Cadence note — you're closer to target than it looks

Your Garmin reports cadence as a per-leg count (68–73), so your actual step rate is roughly 136–146 spm — and Wednesday hit 146. That's solid progress from the 127 of weeks ago, closing in on the 150 intermediate target. Keep the metronome on. Next milestone after 150 is working toward 170 over the coming months.
