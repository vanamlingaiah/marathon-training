The deal that keeps you healthy: split your intensity

You want two group rides and to focus more on running — great, and both fit. But you can't run every session hard. Last week was three hard sessions on an injury-prone knee, 16 weeks out from your first marathon. The fix isn't to drop the fun — it's to let the bikes be the hard days and bring the runs back to easy.

🚴 Bikes = your hard days
Tue & Thu group C rides bring the intensity. Cycling is low-impact, so your knee tolerates hard efforts far better here than on foot. This is the right place for Zone 3–4.
🏃 Runs = your easy base
Every run this week ≤136 bpm. "Focus more on running" = more easy miles, not faster ones. This builds the aerobic engine Houston needs, and keeps the knee quiet.
🎯

The one number that matters this week: run HR ≤136

Saturday's run averaged 156 — that's 20 beats over your Zone 2 ceiling. In Zone 2 your pace will feel almost embarrassingly slow (13:00–13:30/mi), and that is exactly correct. Slow the runs right down, walk when HR climbs, and let the pace be whatever keeps you ≤136. Your fitness shows up as a lower HR at the same pace over the coming weeks — not by running hard now. This single change is what stands between you and an October knee flare.

🦵

Protect the knee — ice + cadence + honest check-ins

Keep the cadence win (155+, build toward 160 — shorter, quicker steps cut patellar load). Ice the right knee 15 min after every run, no matter how it feels. And be honest with yourself mid-run: any knee pain ≥3/10, stop and walk it in — swap to the bike if needed. After a week of all-hard sessions, the first easy run should also be a diagnostic: does the knee feel loaded? Tell me if so.

🚴

On the two group rides — don't redline both

Two group rides is a solid intensity load. But both at Zone 4–5 (like last Thursday) is a lot of near-max work weekly. Try to make one of them a "sit in and spin" ride — stay with the group but let them do the pulling, keep cadence 80+, and don't chase every surge. Save the harder effort for the other day. And if the knee is at all cranky, the bike is where hard efforts are safest — but ease the pushing if fatigue is stacking up.
