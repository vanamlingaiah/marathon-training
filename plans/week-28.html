Why the long run moves to Friday this week

First swim sessions are deceptively tiring — new muscles (lats, shoulders, neck), new breathing patterns, and more core work than you expect. Stacking it on top of a 65-minute long run the same morning would compromise both. So: long run Friday 6:15 AM, swim fresh on Saturday, full rest Sunday. One week only — if the swim becomes a regular slot, we'll restructure properly.

🏊 Private triathlon swim session
The third discipline begins. Technique day — not a fitness test.
What to expect: body position, breathing rhythm, relaxed freestyle basics. A good coach will have you doing drills that feel "too easy" — they're not. Position and breath ARE swimming.
Effort: conversational. If you're gasping, slow down — gasping in water means technique broke, not fitness.
Tell the coach: marathon base + Olympic tri goal + any water-comfort history. Let them set the progression.
🍎 Pre-swim fueling
90 min before: light meal — toast + banana + honey, or half an oats jar. Nothing heavy within an hour — a full stomach and horizontal swimming don't mix. Hydrate normally; you sweat in the pool more than you notice.

Swim Session — Pack List & First-Timer Notes

Pack Thursday evening so Friday stays a recovery evening. Everything fits in one gym bag.

🎒 Pack (Thursday night)
  • Goggles — the one non-negotiable. Anti-fog, snug but not tight. If buying: a basic Speedo/TYR pair is fine, skip the fancy ones for now.
  • Swim cap — many pools require one; keeps hair out of goggles
  • Jammers or snug trunks — board shorts drag badly and make learning harder
  • Towel + flip flops + toiletries
  • Water bottle — poolside, sip between drills
  • Post-swim snack in the bag (banana + protein bar)
🧠 First-timer truths
  • Your run/bike fitness won't transfer yet — swim fitness is technique-first. Expect to feel like a beginner. Everyone does.
  • The #1 skill: exhale UNDERWATER, continuously. Holding your breath is what makes swimming exhausting.
  • Slower is better — drills at easy effort build the stroke; thrashing hard reinforces bad habits.
  • HR behaves differently in water (horizontal + pressure + cooling) — don't chase zones today. The Fenix can track it, but ignore the numbers this session.
  • If your HRM-600 isn't swim-paired, wrist HR is fine for a technique day.

🔫 Massage Gun Protocol — your new recovery layer

Used right, it takes the edge off soreness (DOMS) and keeps muscles supple between sessions. Used wrong — parked on a joint or hammering the knee — it irritates exactly what we're protecting. The routine by session type:

🏃 After runs (6–10 min)
  • Quads · 90 sec/leg
  • Calves · 60–90 sec/leg
  • Hamstrings · 60 sec/leg
  • Glutes · 60 sec/side
  • Speed · medium
  • Pressure · float, don't press
🚴 After bikes (4 min)
  • Quads · 60 sec/leg
  • Glutes · 60 sec/side
  • Hip flexors · 30 sec/side, LIGHT
  • Best time · evening, pre-bed
🏊 After swims (5 min)
  • Lats · 60 sec/side
  • Rear delts · 45 sec/side
  • Upper traps · 45 sec/side
  • Speed · LOWEST — new muscles
  • Never · front of neck/throat
⛔ The rules that protect you
  • Never on the kneecap, joint line, or patellar tendon — your PFPS knee gets zero direct gun contact. Work the quad muscle above it and the outer/inner thigh beside it; the knee benefits indirectly.
  • Keep it moving — slow sweeps along the muscle, never parked on one spot; never on bone or spine.
  • Soreness vs pain: the gun is for dull, both-legs muscle soreness. Sharp, localized, or one-sided joint pain is a signal to REST and tell me — not to massage harder. Especially the right knee.
  • Timing: within 60 min post-workout and/or evening before bed. 2 minutes max per muscle group.
