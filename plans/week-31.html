Why your HR is spiking — and why it's not a problem

Your observation was spot-on: HR hits 140 fast and you're walking more. Here's the truth — that's your body cooling itself, not failing. In heat, blood is diverted to the skin to shed heat, so the heart beats faster to deliver the same oxygen. HR rises sooner and higher at the same effort. Walking more to hold HR down is the run-walk method working perfectly. The proof it's heat, not fitness: your cooler, easy Monday run held normal cadence and pace at HR 133. The fix is earlier starts, pre-cooling, and sodium — and accepting that summer pace is slow on purpose. The engine you're building in this oven will show up dramatically faster in cool January air.

⏰

The single biggest fix: get back to 5:30 AM

Your starts crept to 6:35 and 6:48 this week — and that's a big deal in July. Every 30 minutes earlier is measurably cooler, less humid air. You already proved you can wake early — let's reclaim it now that heat is the enemy.

🌡️ Heat Adaptation Protocols — Aubrey Summer

Your priority for the next 6–8 weeks. Training through Texas heat is uncomfortable but it builds real fitness — more plasma volume, earlier sweating, lower HR at effort. Do these consistently and the same runs feel easier within 2–3 weeks.

⏰ Timing (biggest lever)
5:30 AM out the door on run days; long run by 5:30, done by ~6:45. Every 30 min earlier = several bpm lower HR. If you can't go early, go indoors — don't run in mid-morning July heat.
❄️ Pre-cooling
Before: 12–16 oz cold/icy fluid + a few min in AC. During: frozen bottle in hand, ice bandana on neck, pour water on head/neck. Cooling the skin lowers both perceived effort and real HR.
🧂 Hydration + sodium
16 oz on waking. 500–700 mg sodium/hr of sweating via electrolyte mix (not plain water). Weigh before/after long runs; replace each lost lb with ~16 oz. Pale-yellow urine = hydrated.
📉 Effort, not pace
Pace is meaningless in summer. A slow run at controlled HR in heat = the same stimulus as a faster cool run. Judge every session by HR + feel. Your fast winter pace is being built underneath these slow summer numbers.
🚶 Walk sooner
Heat setting: run→138 / walk→126 (2 bpm lower than usual) for a bigger cooling buffer. Walking more in heat isn't weakness — it's the method regulating you correctly.
⚠️ Heat-illness red flags
STOP if: dizzy, nauseous, headache, goosebumps or chills in heat, stop sweating, confusion, or HR won't come down on walk breaks. These are serious — cool down immediately, hydrate, don't push through.
