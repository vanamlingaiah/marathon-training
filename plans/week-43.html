The base-building formula — why "easy" is the whole game

Your aerobic base — the engine that carries you through 26.2 — is built almost entirely by easy, high-volume Zone 2, not hard efforts. The magic is consistency: show up, keep it easy, repeat. Over these weeks your Zone 2 pace quietly gets faster (you'll run more, walk less, at the same HR). That's the base growing. Every hard session you *avoid* now is an injury you don't get.

🏃 Treadmill runs — all Zone 2
Set the belt slow enough to hold HR ≤136. Walk breaks = lower the speed. Cadence 155+, 1% incline, fan on. The belt won't let you drift slower, so YOU control it.
🚴 Bike — easy D-pace only
13–15 mph, HR in Zone 2. This is a recovery/aerobic day now — no chasing, no redlining. Cover your face for pollen + to warm the air. Spin 80+, don't grind.
🤒

Before anything — is the cold fully gone?

You were sick and on meds this week. Only start this plan once you're recovered — no fever, symptoms above-the-neck gone, energy back. If you're still run-down Monday/Tuesday, push everything later in the week or rest more; a few days don't matter 13 weeks out. And while still recovering, your HR runs high at easy effort — so go by feel (easy = easy), slow the belt, and don't chase pace.

🦵

Back + knee — strength returns gently

Your back flared this week (hard ride + 7 hr/day sitting). So strength comes back, but gently and back-focused: the McGill Big 3 (curl-up, side plank, bird dog) + glutes — the exact fix for both your back and knee. And the daily habit that matters most: break up your sitting every 30–45 min at work (stand, walk, hip-flexor stretch). That does more for your back than any single exercise.

🏃 Treadmill Zone 2 — the key skill this block

The one thing to master: on a treadmill, the belt holds a fixed speed, so you can't slow down naturally when you tire. YOU have to manage it. Here's how.

Do thisWhy
Set belt SLOW to startEasier to speed up than to realize you're in Zone 3. Begin ~4.0–4.5 mph and adjust.
Watch HR, not paceTreadmill pace reads faster than it feels. The only number that matters is HR ≤136.
HR creeps over 136 → drop speedDon't tough it out — lower the belt 0.3–0.5 mph until HR settles. This is the whole skill.
Walk breaks = lower the beltYour run-walk still works: drop to a walk speed (~3.0–3.3), then back up to run.
1% incline + fan1% matches outdoor effort; a fan stops you overheating (no breeze indoors).
Cadence 155+The belt already helped you hit 150 — keep nudging. Quick, light, quiet feet protect the knee.
