🦵 Desk Mobility Routine — for the lower back + knee

Your knee discomfort shows up only when sitting — that's desk stiffness, not a running injury. This routine breaks up the static load that causes it and the lower-back ache. Do the "every break" moves through the workday; do the "twice daily" set morning and evening.

Every 30–45 min (60–90 sec)
at your desk
Seated knee extensions × 10/leg (straighten slowly, hold 2–3s) · stand + march 20–30 steps or walk for water · standing hip-flexor opener 20–30s/side (tuck tailbone, squeeze glute).
Twice daily (5 min)
morning + evening
Glute bridges 2×15 (squeeze 2s) · posterior pelvic tilts 12–15 (flatten low back, hold 5s) · half-kneel hip-flexor stretch 30s/side · clamshells 2×15/side.
Chair setup
reduce the load
Feet flat, knees ~90° (footrest if needed) · lumbar support (rolled towel behind low back) · don't tuck the knee sharply under the chair for long · alternate sit/stand every 30–45 min if you have the standing desk.

When to escalate: this routine is for mild, sitting-only discomfort. If the knee starts hurting during runs, becomes sharp, or you notice swelling, stop training it and see a physical therapist — they can assess kneecap tracking directly. Tell me too, and we'll adjust the plan.
